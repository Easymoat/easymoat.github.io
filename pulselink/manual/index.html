PulseLink — User Manual

Every screen, menu and setting. For PulseLink v3.0.0.

1. Getting Started

Install the app and open it. There is nothing to fill in: your watch carries a Device ID, and PulseLink uses it to identify you. A 14-day free trial of the full version starts the first time you open the app, with no card and no sign-up.

  1. Install PulseLink on your watch from the Connect IQ Store.
  2. Set your name in the Connect IQ phone app: PulseLink settings → Nickname. It supports Japanese and other 2-byte text. The watch shows the first 10 characters.
  3. Open PulseLink. The first screen is your list. On your own, it shows just you.
  4. To connect with someone, ask for their Device ID (Settings → Device ID on their watch), then press START on the list → Connections → Invite. They accept on their watch, and you each appear in the other's list (chapter 3).

Coming from an earlier version

Crews and the Leader role are gone. Your Crew carries over by itself the first time you open this version: each pair in it becomes a connection, and a Crew of three or more also becomes a group with the Crew's name. The level of each connection is the widest one you were both already sharing, so nobody sees more of you than before. Images and the phone camera are no longer shared with other people.

If you used PulseLink for free, you get one free trial of the full version from the first time you open this version. See chapter 13 for what happens when it ends.

2. The List

The list is the first screen: you, the people you are connected with, and your groups. Three buttons at the top open Radar, Image and Camera. A small clock sits above them and the date below the list.

What a row shows

  • Top line — the name on the left and heart rate on the right (from level + Heart).
  • Bottom line — distance walked today on the left, temperature and weather in the middle, steps on the right.
  • Heart rate color — white, orange from 130 bpm, red from 160 bpm.
  • Red name — an unread message from that person or group. The date also changes to a red New Message.
  • -- km and -- st — that person is offline: the app is closed, or the watch is out of connection.
  • A group row — the group name and (online / total), for example Family (3/5). Groups are listed below the people.

Interactions

  • Tap your own row — a Map of just you.
  • Tap a person's row — a Map centered on that person, with you on it too. It shows people at level + Location only.
  • Tap a group row — a Map of that group.
  • Long-press a row — its menu (below).
  • Tap the date, or swipe left — Radar.
  • UP / DOWN (or swipe) — scroll the list.
  • START — open Settings, and send and fetch the latest data at the same time.
  • BACK — close the app (from a group, back to the main list).

Long-press menus

  • A person — Send message / Move to... (change their place in your list) / Remove (end the connection; asks once to confirm).
  • Yourself — goes straight to choosing your place in the list.
  • A group — Open (see its members) / Message group / Move to... / Lower level / Leave (asks once to confirm).
  • A member inside a group — Message group / Move to...

Settings

START on the list opens Settings: Connections (chapter 3), Alerts (chapter 10), Update interval and Device ID (chapter 15).

Update interval is how often your watch sends and fetches: 10 sec, 30 sec (the default), 60 sec, 5 min, 10 min or Manual. Faster uses more battery. With Manual nothing is sent until you press START on the list.

3. Connections and Levels

A connection is between two people, and it starts only when both agree. You can connect with up to 19 other people, 20 including you. There is no leader: each person controls their own connections.

Levels: what you show each other

LevelYou show each other
Activitysteps, distance, weather
+ HeartActivity + heart rate
+ Location+ Heart + your position, on the Radar and the Map

The level belongs to the connection, so it is the same for both of you, and one person never sees more than the other. Your name, whether you are online, and messages work at every level. Your watch sends your position only while you have someone at + Location.

Inviting someone

  1. Ask for their Device ID (Settings → Device ID on their watch). They must have opened PulseLink at least once.
  2. START on the list → Connections → Invite, and enter the ID.
  3. Choose the level: Activity, + Heart or + Location.
  4. A banner tells you the result, for example Invitation sent or ID not found. An invitation expires after a day.

Accepting an invitation

A New invitation banner appears on your watch; the watch checks about every 30 seconds. Open it from START on the list → Connections → Invitations. Pick the invitation: the screen shows what you will share. Choose Accept or Decline. That is your only decision; you never have to set up sharing.

Changing a level

  • Lower — Connections → Change level → the person → a lower level. Either person can do it at any time. It applies to both of you straight away, with no approval.
  • Raise — the same path, but the other person has to accept, just like an invitation.

Ending a connection

Long-press the person's row → Remove. One person is enough, and you disappear from each other's lists straight away. You can invite them again later.

Connections need the full version on both watches. If the other person has none, you see They need the full version.

4. Groups

A group lets several people see each other and talk together. You can be in up to 10 groups, and a group holds up to 20 people including you. Someone can be in several groups at once.

Creating a group

  1. START on the list → Connections → Create group, and enter a name.
  2. Set Level and switch on the people to invite, from your connections.
  3. Choose Create. Each person gets an invitation showing the level, and accepts or declines.

People are invited when the group is created. Joining means connecting with everyone in the group at its level. Leaving ends those connections.

Using a group

  • Tap the row — a Map of the group's members. People at + Location appear on it.
  • Long-press → Open — the list of members. Their data follows the group's level, and the group name is colored by level: blue Activity, green + Heart, orange + Location. BACK returns to the main list.
  • Message group — a message to everyone in the group.
  • Lower level — any member can lower the group's level, and it drops for everyone at once. It cannot be raised; create a new group instead.
  • Leave — only you can take yourself out of a group. No one can remove anyone else.

Levels are kept per list: the main list shows each person at your one-to-one level, and a group shows them at the group's level. Someone you know only through a group does not appear in the main list.

5. Map

The Map shows people on a simple line map with roads, rivers and terrain. Open it by tapping a row in the list. Only people at + Location appear on it.

  • UP / DOWN (or swipe) — UP, or swiping up, widens the range; DOWN, or swiping down, narrows it. There are 10 steps from 200 m to 102 km. Your choice is remembered.
  • Long-press a marker — center the Map on it. Long-press again to return to yourself.
  • START — the Map menu: Clock, Clock look (chapter 8), Background (Slate, Black, Charcoal, Navy, Forest or Plum) and Center on.
  • BACK — back to the list.

You are the white dot and ring. Other people are green circles with their initial. A position more than 10 minutes old turns grey and shows its age. Until your own watch has a GPS fix, the Map shows GPS...

6. Radar

A rotating compass with a GPS radar on it: your saved places and the people at + Location, with bearing and distance. It is a light just-in-case aid for trail runs, hikes and climbs. Open it from the first button on the list, by tapping the date, or by swiping left on the list.

Interactions

  • UP / DOWN (or swipe) — UP, or swiping up, widens the range; DOWN, or swiping down, narrows it, from 100 m to 1000 km.
  • Tap a marker — change its label: Distance → Name → Marks only.
  • Tap elsewhere — change all the labels.
  • Long-press a marker — center the radar on it. Long-press again to return to yourself.
  • Long-press elsewhere — strip every label to a plain mark, and back. The quickest way to read a crowded radar.
  • START — the Radar menu.
  • BACK — back to the list.

The Radar menu

  • Save here — save your position as a place. Up to 10 places with the full version, 1 on free.
  • Enter coords — save a place you are not standing in. Type latitude and longitude in decimal degrees, separated by a space or a comma (35.6812 139.7671), with a minus sign for south or west. Your position is filled in to start with.
  • Navigate to... — pick a place or a person, and a single big arrow points straight at it, with the live distance.
  • My position — your latitude, longitude and altitude in large text, handy for reading out to rescue services.
  • View — Marks only, Labels, Show (Places + Members, Places only, Members only or Off), Center on, Per marker, and Clear track for the faint trail of where you have been.
  • A saved place — Show on radar, Rename, Delete and Alert.

Alerts on the Radar

  • Arrive / leave — choose a saved place and set Alert. Your watch vibrates and shows a banner when someone comes within 100 m or moves beyond 150 m: a child reaching school, for example. It needs PulseLink open on your watch and that person at + Location. Full version only.
  • Crew distance — Settings → Alerts → Crew distance tells you when someone at + Location passes a fixed distance from you, in either direction: 100 m, 200 m, 500 m, 1 km, 2 km, 3 km, 4 km, 5 km, 10 km, 20 km and 50 km. The banner reads Alice 1km+ as they leave and Alice within 1km as they come back. It is off by default.
North-up ("N up") — why it happens: To save power, the watch stops feeding the magnetic-compass (magnetometer) heading once its high-power display session times out — the same reason Garmin's built-in compass dims itself. When that happens the dial can no longer follow your heading, so the radar automatically switches to a fixed North-up view (marked "N up") instead of freezing on a stale frame.

Which watches: This mainly affects AMOLED watches, and larger high-resolution screens (such as the Forerunner 965) reach it sooner because their bright display costs more power. Smaller-screen models rarely hit it. Either way, your bearings and distances stay accurate — only the dial's rotation stops. Open the compass again to resume rotation.
Known behavior — compass may freeze: Due to Garmin's CPU budget for widget-type apps, if the compass updates rapidly for an extended period (e.g. while moving your arm continuously), the system may suspend its drawing thread. The screen will freeze on the last frame. This is normal Garmin power management behavior, not a crash.

To recover: Press BACK to leave the Radar, then restart PulseLink (close and reopen). The compass works again.

7. Image

The Image screen is your picture with your day on top of it. Open it from the middle button on the list. Add pictures on the image page; a slot with no picture shows the PulseLink logo.

Open the image page →

The menu (START)

  • Clock — off shows a small clock and the date at the top; on shows the full clock (Hands, Rim marks, or Digital large in the middle).
  • Clock look — how the clock looks (chapter 8). Appears when Clock is on.
  • Image — off gives a black background with just the information. The Slot row is then hidden.
  • Slot — choose Slot 1 to 5 (5 with the full version).

The information

Sixteen values appear in six rows and update every 30 seconds. A value the watch cannot read shows --.

RowValues
1weather and temperature / pressure
2humidity / wind direction and speed / chance of rain
3altitude / heart rate / steps
4floors climbed / calories / weight
5Body Battery / stress / respiration
6moon and its age / next sunrise or sunset

The wind arrow is a kite shape: the pointed end shows where the wind is heading, the notched end where it comes from. Weight comes from Garmin, and values such as Body Battery appear only on watches that have them. BACK, or swiping right, returns to the list.

8. Clock Look

Image, Camera and Map can each show a clock. Clock in each screen's menu turns it on or off for that screen only. Clock look sets how it looks, and it is the same on every screen.

  • Clock style — Hands (the default), Rim marks or Digital.
  • Clock color — one color for the hands, ticks, digital time and date. Default is blue; or choose Blue, Cyan, Green, Yellow, Orange, Red, Pink, Purple, Green-Yellow, Gray, Black or White. It also sets the clock and date on the list.
  • Clock ticks — tick marks around the edge, on by default.
  • Halo / Halo color — an outline around the hands and digital time, so they stand out over a picture. Off by default. It is not used while the screen is dimmed.
  • Rim meters — the meters along the left and right edges, on Image and Camera. The list always shows them.
  • Left / Right rim meter — their colors. The left meter is the battery. The right is Body Battery or Stress, chosen in the phone settings.
  • Battery Saver — dims the screen to a black background with just the clock and date after 5, 10 or 20 seconds with no touch (Off by default). On Camera and Map it works when Clock is on. It does not run on the Radar.
  • Saver Wake — what wakes the screen from dimming: Motion or Tap.

9. Camera (PulseLink Cam)

Turn a spare phone into a still camera and see it on your wrist: a pet in another room, or the garden while you are out. It needs no app, because the phone just keeps a web page open. The Camera needs the full version, and it shows only on your own watch.

Setting up the camera phone

  1. Plug the phone into power and place it where it can see what you want to watch.
  2. Open easymoat.com/camera in the browser (Safari on iPhone) and sign in with your Device ID (Settings → Device ID on the watch). If the page asks for a Web code, read it from the same menu (chapter 15).
  3. Press Start camera. A dashed circle shows the round area your watch displays. Aim the phone until your subject sits inside it.
  4. Leave the page open. It keeps the screen awake by itself; if the status line says the wake lock is off, set Auto-Lock to Never on the phone. Darken screen blacks out the display while the camera keeps running.
  5. On the watch, press the Camera button on the list.

On the watch

  • Change the angle — tap the top, bottom, left or right edge of the screen. An Angle banner shows the position, and the camera follows within about 10-30 seconds. The view moves along the long side of the frame.
  • START — the Camera menu: Clock, Clock look, Zoom (1.0x to 2.5x), Brightness (-2 to +2), Night mode (Auto, On or Off) and Motion alert (off, vibe only, or vibe + tone).
  • BACK, or swipe right — back to the list.

The Camera and the Image screen are separate. Going back and forth does not change which photo slot is chosen.

Reading the picture

  • Capture time — the time at the top is when the photo was taken, not the current time. It turns yellow once the picture is more than 30 minutes old, so a frozen image never passes for the current view.
  • How fresh it is — the picture on your wrist is typically a few minutes old. Transferring an image over Bluetooth is what sets this pace.
  • Night vision — dark photos are automatically brightened and shown in night-vision green. Pitch darkness stays black, so a small night light makes a big difference.

Viewer — watch and adjust from any screen

easymoat.com/pulselink/view shows the same camera on any phone, tablet or PC, at full quality. Sign in with the same Device ID (and the Web code, when asked). You can pause and resume, shoot now, and set zoom, brightness, colors, the camera phone's screen and night vision. Changes usually reach the camera in 10-30 seconds.

What you need

  • The camera phone — iPhone 5s or newer (iPhone 8 or newer recommended, so the page can keep the screen awake by itself), or an Android phone with Chrome. On older iPhones use Safari — other browsers could not reach the camera before iOS 14.3.
  • Power and a foreground page — keep the phone charging with the page in front. Phones stop the camera when a page goes to the background; that is an operating-system rule, not a PulseLink limit.
  • A light on the watch — the phone's torch can be switched on from the Viewer, but only on iOS 17.5+ or Android; on older iPhones the web has no access to the light and the button is greyed out.

10. Messages and Alerts

Sending

Long-press a person → Send message, or a group → Message group. Pick one of your templates, or Type a message... for free text. The templates are Message 1-20 in the phone settings; empty ones are skipped. The address is set by where you opened it from.

Receiving

A message arrives as a banner across the screen, with the sender and the text. Tap it (or press START) to reply straight away, or tap outside it or press another button to close it. It stays until you do. An unread message also turns the name red and the date into New Message.

Sending a message opens the message feed, newest first. Tap an entry to reply to that conversation. Opening the feed clears the unread marks. Messages are deleted automatically after 24 hours.

Alerts

Settings → Alerts. Msg & Invite decides how messages and invitations reach your wrist: off (the default), vibe + tone, or vibe only. Choosing one plays a preview. The banner appears whichever you choose. Crew distance is described in chapter 6.

11. Distance on Any Watch Face

PulseLink offers two complications for your watch's own watch face. They appear as PulseLink Crew 1 and PulseLink Crew 2 in the data field list, and show the distance to the first two people in your main list. To change who appears, use Move to... in the list.

  • They keep updating about every 5 minutes, even with PulseLink closed.
  • Only people at + Location have a distance. Anyone else, or a position more than 15 minutes old, shows --.
  • Long-press the field to open the Radar.

12. Watches Without a Touchscreen

Everything is done with the buttons. UP and DOWN scroll the list or change the range on the Map and Radar, START opens the menu, and BACK goes back. Hold UP for the selection frame, which does the work of a tap or long-press.

ButtonWith the selection frame
Hold UPshow the orange frame, with the action name under it
UP / DOWNmove to the next choice, going round
STARTdo the action named under the frame
BACKhide the frame
  • The list — the Radar, Image and Camera buttons, then each row. A row has two actions: it shows Map first, then Menu (Move to for your own row), then moves on to the next row.
  • Radar — each marker: Label, then Center. Everything else is in the menu, for example Marks only and Labels.
  • Map — each marker: Center.
  • Camera — Up, Right, Down and Left move the angle.
  • Image — no frame.
  • A message banner — close it with START, which opens the reply; then press BACK if you only wanted to read it. Other buttons keep their usual job and leave the banner up.

14. Weather and Your Watch's Sensors

PulseLink reads the weather Garmin has already put on your watch, synced from your phone. It appears on each row of the list, and in full on the Image screen. Clear and partly cloudy skies show a moon at night, decided by sunrise and sunset where you are.

Units

Choose the units in the Connect IQ phone app → PulseLink settings: Temperature unit (Auto, °C or °F), Pressure unit (hPa, inHg or mmHg), Altitude unit (Auto, m or ft), Wind speed unit (Auto, m/s, km/h, mph or knots) and Weight unit (Auto, kg or lb). Auto follows your watch's own unit settings; for wind speed, Auto means mph when the watch measures distance in miles and m/s when it uses kilometres.

The date reads Sun 10/11 by default. To change it: PulseLink settings → Date format (Oct 11 Sun, 10/11 Sun, 11 Oct Sun or Sun 10/11).

⚠ Your watch's sensor mode — important in the mountains

Your watch has one barometric sensor, and many models let you decide how it reads pressure changes — Auto / Altimeter only / Barometer only (Settings → Sensors → Altimeter, or Barometer → Sensor Mode; the exact path varies by model, e.g. Venu 4: Settings → System → Altimeter & Barometer → Sensor Mode). This setting is part of the watch, not PulseLink, but it directly changes what PulseLink can show you.

Do not use "Barometer only" with the radar. In that mode the watch freezes your altitude and reads every pressure change as weather. Your altitude stops changing as you climb, so the altitude saved with a waypoint and the altitude on My position stop being meaningful. Distances and bearings still work — only the altitude does not.
  • Auto — best for everyday use: the watch decides: pressure changes count as altitude while you move, and as weather while you are still. Everything in PulseLink works. This is the right choice unless you have a reason to change it.
  • Altimeter only — best for hiking, trail runs and climbs: every pressure change is treated as altitude, so your altitude follows the climb quickly and reliably. The trade-off: while you stay in one place for a long time (a long rest, a night at camp), passing weather slowly drifts your altitude, so re-calibrate at a known elevation.
  • Barometer only — avoid while using PulseLink outdoors: altitude is frozen, as described above. The pressure reading and its 3-hour trend (rising / falling) stay correct in every mode, so you do not need this mode to watch the weather.

No Sensor Mode on your watch? (Venu 3 / 3S and similar) — nothing to worry about. Those watches still have a barometric altimeter; they simply manage it for you, which behaves like Auto, and every PulseLink feature works normally. There is no mode to avoid. What matters on these models is calibration: Settings → Watch Sensors → Barometer → Calibrate, where you can enter a known elevation or let the watch calibrate from GPS.

15. Your Device ID

Your watch carries a Device ID, and PulseLink uses it to identify you. Find it at START on the list → Settings → Device ID. You need it for the website and for purchasing.

  • The ID row — XXXX-XXXX, with your trial days or Full version beside it. Select it to show it large. It stays the same if you reinstall the app.
  • Web code — the website asks for a 6-digit code. Choose Web code, and enter the digits on the page within 10 minutes. You stay signed in for 30 days on the image, viewer and deletion pages, and 90 days on the camera page.
  • Sign out web — signs out every web page and camera phone that used this watch, all at once, for example when you lend or lose a phone.
  • Background and Build — status and version numbers, useful when you contact us.
  • Web tools: easymoat.com/pulselink/tools
  • Delete my data: easymoat.com/pulselink/delete

16. Troubleshooting

Find your symptom below and work down its list in order. The first item is the answer more often than not.

Try this first: Close PulseLink and open it again. Your connections, images and settings are safe, and it clears a surprising number of problems.

A. Getting connected

Stuck on "Connecting..."

  • Check Bluetooth — the watch has no internet of its own and borrows your phone's.
  • Check that Connect IQ Mobile is installed on the phone and allowed to run in the background.
  • Check that the phone itself has a working connection — try loading a web page on it.
  • Walk closer to the phone. Bluetooth drops out at range even when both say they are paired.

Everything looks empty after reinstalling the app

  • Wait a few seconds after opening it. Your connections, images and settings live on the server and are fetched on startup.
  • Your Device ID does not change when you reinstall, so nothing was lost.
  • If it is still empty after a restart, check Bluetooth. A watch that cannot reach the server shows just you.

My Device ID does not work on the website

  • Read it from the watch rather than from memory: Settings → Device ID.
  • It is 8 characters with a hyphen in the middle (XXXX-XXXX). Case does not matter, and the hyphen is added for you if you leave it out.
  • 0 and O, and 1 and I, are easy to confuse. Try the other one.
  • "Not entitled" on the image or camera page means the watch has no active trial or licence. Open PulseLink on the watch to start the trial, or purchase.

The website asks for a Web code

  • Your watch is registered, so the page needs proof that you have it. On the watch, open Settings → Device ID, choose Web code and type the 6 digits within 10 minutes.
  • The code expired: choose Web code again for a fresh one.
  • "Registered elsewhere. See Device ID" on the watch means this Device ID is already registered from another watch. Open the Device ID menu for what to do; if you replaced your watch, email easymoat@gmail.com.

I use two watches

  • Each watch has its own Device ID, and everything belongs to one ID: your connections, images and camera.
  • Something that seems to have disappeared is usually on the other watch's ID. On the website, sign in with the ID of the watch you are holding.
  • A licence covers one watch. If you replaced your watch, the new one has a new ID and needs to be registered.

B. Connections

The invitation never arrived

  • Check the Device ID you gave. ID not found on the sender's watch means it was mistyped, or that watch has never opened PulseLink.
  • The watch checks about every 30 seconds, so give it a moment. Look in Settings → Connections → Invitations. Invitations expire after a day.
  • They need the full version means the other watch has no trial or licence. Full version required means yours does not.

Someone vanished from my list

  • They may have ended the connection, or their trial or subscription may have ended (chapter 13). Invite them again.
  • Check Bluetooth first: a watch that cannot reach the server shows just you, with everything intact.

C. Live data

Someone shows "--" or never updates

  • -- km and -- st mean they are offline: app closed, or out of Bluetooth range.
  • A blank heart rate means your level with them is Activity (chapter 3).
  • With Update interval set to Manual, nothing moves until START is pressed. Set an interval in Settings.
  • A watch only sends while PulseLink is open or running in the background. A watch asleep on a desk simply ages.

D. Radar and Map: someone does not appear

Check these in order

  • Is your level with them + Location? Only that level shows a position. Settings → Connections → Change level shows it; raising it needs their acceptance.
  • Is the radar set to show Members? Radar menu → View → Show must be Places + Members or Members only.
  • Does your own watch have a GPS fix? Until it does the screen shows GPS... A first fix outdoors takes 30-60 seconds.
  • Can their watch see the sky? Indoors, in a car or under heavy tree cover it may never get a fix.
  • Are they beyond your current range? Anyone farther than the ring is a small dot on the rim, direction only. Widen the range with UP (or swipe up).

Someone is grey, with a time like "12m"

This is deliberate. Their position has not refreshed for over 10 minutes, so it is greyed and dated rather than shown as current. It means this is where they last were.

A position and distance never move

  • On some watch models the watch's own GPS can stop working, so PulseLink has no new position to send. This is not a PulseLink fault.
  • On the watch whose position is not moving, open an activity that uses GPS and wait until it acquires a signal.

E. Screens and sensors

The compass froze on the last frame

  • This is Garmin's CPU budget protection, not a crash. See chapter 6.
  • Press BACK and open it again.

A value shows "--" on the Image screen

  • Weather values need Garmin Connect on your phone to sync them to the watch. Open it.
  • A body value may not be supported on your watch model, or may have no reading yet. Wear the watch snugly and give it a few minutes.

F. Trial and licence

I purchased but the watch still says the trial ended

  • Check that the Device ID you registered matches the watch in your hand: Settings → Device ID.
  • Check that the key is in PulseLink settings → License Key, then restart PulseLink. The watch only checks when the app starts.

Still stuck?

Write to easymoat@gmail.com with your Device ID, the Build number in Settings → Device ID, and what you saw.
